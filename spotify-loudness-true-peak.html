Why a Loud Master Can Sound Quieter on Spotify: LUFS and True Peak

Why a track with a higher measured loudness does not necessarily sound bigger than the reference — and why inter-sample peaks matter when mastering.

You made your master louder than the reference. On Spotify, it sounds quieter and smaller. Why?

Streaming platforms use loudness normalization. Spotify’s default playback setting targets −14 LUFS. That means your −6 LUFS master will simply be turned down. And once its level is reduced, an over-limited track can sound smaller than a more dynamic one.

The second issue is peaks. You may see 0 dB on the clipper, but after encoding for streaming, inter-sample peaks can exceed zero. That can introduce distortion or harsh artifacts.

That is why I enable True Peak in Pro-L 2. I set the master ceiling to −1 dBTP, or −2 dBTP for a master louder than −14 LUFS. Spotify also recommends leaving headroom to help avoid clipping during encoding and playback.
