“Chervonyi Mak” and Suno AI: Is One Click Enough to Create a Hit?

The song “Chervonyi Mak” has attracted considerable attention in Ukraine. It is a useful starting point for thinking about AI-assisted music and the role of the human creator.

I doubt that creating truly competitive music always comes down to pressing a single button in Suno. At the same time, without reliable information about a particular song’s production, we cannot claim to know exactly how it was made. So let’s look at the broader question: what helps turn an AI generation into a personal piece of music?

What Goes Into an Interesting Musical Idea?

A finished composition may involve an original idea, lyrics, vocal melody, rhythm, mood, arrangement and many creative decisions. Suno AI can help turn a concept into sound, but a person often guides the process: choosing what to keep, what to change and what result to pursue.

Generation can be a useful starting point — a way to test an idea, explore a mood or create a demo. But the first output does not have to be the final version.

The More of Yourself You Bring In, the More Distinctive the Result Can Be

Imagine two creators. One generates a track and publishes it immediately. The other experiments with the idea, revises the lyrics, explores vocal melodies, selects the strongest parts and develops the arrangement. Both use the same tool, but their creative approaches differ.

Your taste, experience, aesthetic choices and decisions as a songwriter or producer help shape an individual result. AI can be a powerful tool, but it does not remove the value of a creator’s vision.

What Can You Improve After Generating a Track in Suno?

Even a promising generation may need more work before release. Depending on the material, this can include changes to the structure and arrangement, editing individual parts, sound shaping, professional mixing or mastering.

  • Arrangement: song development, instrumental parts and transitions.
  • Mixing: balancing vocals and instruments into a cohesive mix.
  • Mastering: preparing the finished mix for release.

Mastering does not replace arrangement work, and mixing cannot automatically fix every limitation in the source material. It is best to assess the track first and decide which steps are actually needed.

Keep Music Platform Policies in Mind

If you plan to release music on streaming platforms, check the current rules of the specific service and distributor regarding AI use, rights to the material and content requirements. Policies can differ and change, so it is better to verify them than rely on assumptions.
