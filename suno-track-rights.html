Suno Track Rights: What You Can and Cannot Do

Understand Suno’s commercial licence, free versus paid plans, and the difference between usage rights and copyright protection.

Before releasing a track made with Suno, separate two questions: what the service allows you to do with the audio, and whether copyright law protects the material. A commercial-use licence from a platform is not the same thing as a copyright registration or a guarantee that every element is legally exclusive.

Why the subscription matters

Under Suno's terms effective in September 2026, commercial-use rights are associated with songs generated and downloaded while an eligible paid subscription is active. Songs made on the free plan are for personal, non-commercial use. Keep a copy of the applicable terms and evidence of your subscription and download date.

Keep a clear record

  • Save the original audio, stems and project files.
  • Record the generation date and the subscription tier used.
  • Keep prompts, edits, lyrics and other material you created yourself.
  • Check the terms again before each release; platform rules can change.

Commercial permission is not copyright protection

Permission under Suno's terms does not automatically mean that a fully AI-generated recording qualifies for copyright protection. Copyright rules differ by country and often focus on identifiable human creative contribution. Original lyrics, a melody written by a person, live performances, substantial arrangement decisions and production work may matter, depending on the applicable law and facts.

Do not assume that a paid plan makes an unauthorised voice clone or borrowed copyrighted material safe to release. Avoid prompts intended to imitate a specific performer, clear samples and third-party material properly, and obtain consent where needed. For a significant commercial release or a dispute, get advice from a qualified music-rights lawyer.

Bottom line: use an eligible paid plan, download while it is active, preserve evidence and add meaningful human authorship. Treat the service licence and copyright ownership as separate issues.
