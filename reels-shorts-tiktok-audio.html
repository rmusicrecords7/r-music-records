Audio for Reels, Shorts and TikTok: Mastering for Short-Form Video

Prepare a short excerpt so the hook, vocal and bass translate on phones without excessive limiting.

A song may be discovered through a 15- or 30-second clip before anyone hears the full release. That excerpt has to communicate the hook quickly, but it should still sound like part of the same production. Short-form platforms also re-encode audio, and their delivery behaviour can change, so there is no single fixed loudness number that guarantees the best result.

Choose the right excerpt

Pick a section that works without a long introduction: a vocal line, a distinctive rhythm, a drop or a memorable melodic phrase. Check that the clip has a clear beginning and ending and does not cut off a word or musical gesture awkwardly. If the song needs context, prepare a short lead-in rather than starting in the middle of a phrase.

Make it translate on a phone

Phone speakers reproduce limited low frequencies. Make sure the bass has enough audible harmonics and that the vocal and hook remain clear in the midrange. Check mono compatibility and avoid relying on an ultra-wide stereo effect that disappears on a small device.

Do not over-limit

Platforms may apply their own encoding and playback processing. Excessive limiting can make the clip harsh and tiring without guaranteeing it will play louder. Compare at matched loudness and listen to an encoded upload preview when available.

Prepare versions thoughtfully

Keep the full master as the reference. If you create a separate short-form edit, make sure the transition into and out of the excerpt feels intentional. Check that the chosen section is cleared for use on the platform and that the rights and metadata are correct.

Takeaway: a strong short-form excerpt depends on a memorable musical moment, clear vocal and midrange balance, and sensible dynamics—not an extreme loudness target.
