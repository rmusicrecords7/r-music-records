Working Remotely with a Mixing Engineer: Process and File Security

The stages of remote collaboration, file transfer, pricing and revisions, confidentiality and safe handling of source files.

Remote mixing and mastering lets an artist work with an engineer regardless of city or country. The process can be efficient and secure when the brief, file transfer and approvals are organized before work begins.

Agree on the project first

Describe the goal, send a demo and references, and confirm the scope, fee, deadline and revision policy. Specify which versions are needed: main master, instrumental, clean version, performance track or stems. Put important agreements in writing so both sides know what is included.

Transfer files safely

Use a reputable cloud service or secure transfer link. Keep the session private, limit access to the people involved and avoid public links to unreleased material. Send organized WAV files with consistent start points and a track list. Keep your own backup of every file you deliver.

Protect the material and clarify usage

Discuss confidentiality where necessary and agree whether the engineer may use the finished work in a portfolio. Clarify who owns the recordings, compositions and production elements, and whether any third-party loops or samples are involved. Do not assume that paying for a mix automatically transfers every underlying right.

Manage approvals

Listen to the first version on your systems, send one consolidated revision list and approve the final files only after checking names, formats and versions. Ask how long project files are retained and whether they can be retrieved later.

Takeaway: remote collaboration is reliable when responsibilities, file security, rights and deliverables are clear from the start.
